When is the Best Time to Have Investors?

Most people ask: "When should I raise money?" A better question is: "What problem am I trying to solve with capital?"

The Three Valid Reasons to Raise

  • Speed — You know what to do, you just need to move faster
  • Scale — Demand exists and supply is the bottleneck
  • Risk Reduction — Capital helps you cross a credibility or infrastructure gap

"If capital is funding uncertainty, it usually creates pressure instead of progress."

Healthy timing feels like: Clear priorities, a short list of use cases for the money, and confidence in what happens after the raise.

Unhealthy timing feels like: "Everyone else is raising," "I'm running out of time," or "Investors told me to come back with traction—but I don't know which traction."

How to Pitch to an Investor?

Pitching is translation, not persuasion. Founders speak in vision and possibility. Investors listen for risk, return, timing, and decision-making quality.

The Four Questions Every Pitch Must Answer

  • What problem are you solving—and for whom?
  • Why are you the right person to solve it?
  • Why does this matter now?
  • How does this become a return?

Notice that only one of those is about the idea. The rest are about execution and judgment.

What Investors Actually Screen For: Early-stage investors are not betting on certainty. They're betting on how founders think, how founders learn, and how founders respond to information. A pitch that says "I don't know yet, but here's how I'm testing it" is often stronger than one that pretends everything is solved.

The Danger of Having Investors Too Early

Capital always comes with expectations—even when it's friendly.

The Hidden Costs of Early Capital

  • Dilution Without Value — Giving up equity before you understand your value
  • Wrong Direction — Locking into a growth narrative you can't yet support
  • Time Drain — Spending time reporting instead of building
  • Mental Shift — Once you take money, you stop asking "What's true?" and start asking "What will investors think?"

"That shift can happen before your foundation is strong—and it's hard to undo."

Simple Gut Check: If capital disappeared tomorrow—would you still know what to build next? Would you still know who your customer is? Would you still believe in the business? If the answer is no, capital may be masking a clarity problem.

Can European Startups Receive U.S. Funding?

The short answer: Yes—European startups can absolutely receive U.S. investment.

The real nuance: But it depends on who the investor is, how the investment is structured, and what stage you're at.

Angels vs Institutions

  • U.S. Angels are often comfortable investing directly into European companies—especially early on
  • U.S. Institutions usually require a U.S. entity, often a Delaware company, because of legal and tax constraints

The Biggest Mistake: The mistake I see most often is founders restructuring before capital actually requires it. A U.S. entity is a long-term commitment—with tax, legal, and operational implications.

The Sengo Principle: Structure should follow capital rather than anticipation.
